Optician Essential Cheat Sheet

Chairside Troubleshooting, Transposition, Fitting Heights & Nose Pad Angles
PRACTICAL GUIDE
◆ PRESCRIPTION POWER SWAP (TRANSPOSITION)

1. Main Power (Sphere): Add first two numbers together (+/-).

2. Flip Astigmatism Sign: Change (+) to (-) or (-) to (+). Keep number same.

3. Turn Axis by 90°: If original ≤ 90°, add 90. If > 90°, subtract 90.

Worked Example: -2.00 +1.50 x 045 → -0.50 -1.50 x 135
◆ NOSE PAD ANGLES (HOW TO ANGLE THEM)

Front Angle (~20°): Tops closer together than bottoms to follow nose slope.

Side Angle (Splay ~25°-30°): Front edges closer than backs as nose widens.

Upright Tilt (~10°-15°): Tops tilt forward slightly so pad sits flat without digging.

★ Golden Rule: Entire flat surface must touch skin—never just an edge or corner.
◆ WHERE TO LINE UP BIFOCALS & PROGRESSIVES

No-Line Progressives (PALs): Center of pupil at eye level. 14-18mm below pupil. Cheek tilt 8-12°.

Lined Bifocals: Top line at lower eyelid (lower limbus). Drop 1mm for drivers/hikers.

Trifocals & Office: Upper line at bottom of pupil. Office: Center pupil or +1mm above.

◆ 3 CRUCIAL FRAME ANGLES TO CHECK FIRST

1. Pantoscopic Tilt (8°-12°): Lenses must tilt inward toward cheeks to widen reading channel.

2. Panoramic Face Wrap: Frame must curve slightly around face to avoid fishbowl sides.

3. Vertex Distance (~12mm): Closer without lashes touching widens field of view.

EXPANDED CHAIRSIDE TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE (CUSTOMER COMPLAINTS & BENCH FIXES)
A. Progressive & Multifocal Complaints
CUSTOMER COMPLAINT WHAT IS HAPPENING HOW TO FIX AT THE BENCH
"I have to tilt my chin way up to read my phone or book" Reading sweet spot sitting too low in frame. Squeeze nose pads closer, increase cheek tilt (8-12°), bring lenses closer.
"I have to tuck my chin down to see street signs or TV" Reading portion sitting too high, invading distance zone. Spread nose pads wider to drop frame, reduce cheek tilt slightly.
"My reading area feels like looking through a tiny straw" Glasses sit too far away, or frame sits too upright. Shorten vertex distance (push closer), increase cheek tilt 10-12°, verify PD.
"I have to turn my whole head to read across a single line" Lenses too far from eyes, or pupillary distance was made too wide. Bring frame closer to widen viewing funnel. If channel off-center, remake.
"The bifocal line gets in the way when I walk downstairs" Bifocal line is set too high for walking gait. Widen nose pads to drop frame 1-2mm, bend temple tips so frame sits lower.
B. Distortion, Swim Effect & High Prescriptions
CUSTOMER COMPLAINT WHAT IS HAPPENING HOW TO FIX AT THE BENCH
"The room looks like a fishbowl / walls bend when I turn" Frame is completely flat across face or lenses too far. Add slight face wrap (panoramic curve) and reduce vertex distance.
"The floor feels curved or like I am stepping into a hole" Astigmatism angle or tilt causing vertical spatial distortion. Double-check axis on lensometer. Tilt bottom of frame inward toward cheeks.
"Everything seems slightly double or pulls my eyes" Optical centers do not line up with eyes (unwanted prism). Verify optical center height and PD against prescription; level frame temples.
"Objects look smaller / larger than with old glasses" Big power shift or vertex distance changed. Adjust nose pads to match vertex distance of comfortable previous glasses.
C. Frame Slipping, Sore Spots & Physical Fit
CUSTOMER COMPLAINT WHAT IS HAPPENING HOW TO FIX AT THE BENCH
"My glasses constantly slide down my nose" Bridge too wide, pads not gripping, or ear bend starts too far back. Re-bend temple tips right at ear-skull junction. Squeeze pads closer.
"Pain or sore red marks behind my ears" Temple tips bend too early and press ear cartilage or mastoid bone. Move bend 2-3mm back to hug skull gently; flare very end outward.
"Pinching or deep red indentations on nose" Pads too close or resting on sharp corner/edge. Spread pads slightly wider; rotate so full pad rests flush. Swap to silicone.
"My eyelashes brush against the back of lenses" Glasses sit too close to eyes or lack forward cheek tilt. Lengthen pad arms to move lenses forward, or adjust acetate bridge gap.
"The frame sits crooked—one eye higher than the other" Ears sit at different heights on the head. Golden Rule: Bend temple on the higher side UP (or lower side down).
"Severe reflections, glare, or halos around headlights at night" Missing AR coating, dirty/smeared lenses, or flat tilt catching overhead light. Add anti-reflective (AR) coating. Increase cheek tilt slightly.
